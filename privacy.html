Spoonbook

Privacy policy

Last updated 1 November 2026

Spoonbook is a shared cookbook app run by Ian Worsley, a sole trader trading as Spoonbook (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information Spoonbook keeps, why, and what you can do about it. We’ve kept it short and plain.

The short version

  • We keep your account and the recipes, photos and lists you put in your cookbook, so the app works and so the people you share with can see them.
  • We don’t show adverts, we don’t use analytics or tracking, and we don’t sell or share your information for marketing.
  • Spoonbook doesn’t use AI. Reading recipes, sorting the shopping list and working out calories all happen on your phone.

What we keep

  • Your account: your email address and password. Passwords are stored scrambled (hashed), so nobody can read them, including us.
  • If you sign in with Google: Google tells us your name, email address and profile picture. We use the email to identify your account and suggest your first name for the cookbook. We never see your Google password.
  • Your name in the cookbook: the name you enter, so the people you share with can see who added what.
  • Your cookbook: recipes, notes, ratings, “we made this” dates, photos, meal plans, shopping lists, cupboard staples and usuals. Everyone you’ve invited to your cookbook can see these. People invited to edit can also change them, people invited to view can only look.
  • Feedback: if you send feedback from the app, we keep your message, your first name and which phone or browser you used, so we can reply and fix problems.
  • Recipes you share as a link: when you choose “Send a link”, a copy of that one recipe (with its photo, your note and your first name) is saved so that anyone with the link can view it. The rest of your cookbook stays private. Shared copies are deleted if you delete your account.
  • Payments: Spoonbook is free at the moment. If we introduce a paid plan, we’ll keep only whether your cookbook has it and when it renews. Payments would be taken by a payment provider named here before anything goes on sale, and we’d never see your card number.

Where it’s kept

Your account and cookbook are stored with Supabase, our database and hosting provider, in their [region, e.g. London (eu-west-2)] data centre. The app itself is served by GitHub Pages. Emails from Spoonbook (such as confirming your account or resetting your password) are sent through Resend, and emails you send to us arrive through ImprovMX into our Google mailbox. When someone joins a cookbook, we get an email with their name and email address so we know who is using Spoonbook, and feedback sent from the app reaches us by email too. These providers process information only to run the service for us.

Spoonbook also keeps a copy of your cookbook and photos on your phone, so it opens quickly and works with no signal. Signing out removes the saved recipes from the phone.

Features that touch other services

  • Getting a recipe from a link: the web address you paste is fetched by our server so the recipe and its photo can be read. We keep only the recipe you choose to save.
  • Snapping a cookbook page: the photo is read on your phone. The first time you use it, your phone downloads the reading software from jsDelivr, a public file service, which sees your IP address like any website would.
  • Hands-free cooking: steps are read out and commands are heard using your phone’s built-in speech features. On many Android phones, speech recognition is provided by Google and may send audio to Google to turn it into text, under Google’s own privacy terms. The microphone is only used while hands-free is switched on in cooking mode.
  • Fonts: the app’s typefaces load from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address.
  • Calorie estimates use the UK Composition of Foods Integrated Dataset, built into the app. Nothing is sent anywhere to work them out.

Why we’re allowed to use it

We use your information to provide the app you’ve signed up for (performing our contract with you) and, for payments, to meet legal and tax duties if we ever take payments. We don’t use it for anything else.

How long we keep it

We keep your account and cookbook for as long as you use Spoonbook. Deleted recipes stay in “Recently deleted” for 30 days, then they’re removed. You can delete your account at any time in the app (⋯ → Delete my account). This deletes it straight away, along with your cookbook and its photos, unless other people still share that cookbook, in which case you’re removed from it and the recipes stay with them. You can also ask us to do it by email. We keep weekly backups of the database for up to 12 weeks so we can recover from mistakes, so deleted information can remain in a backup until it's replaced. Payment records are kept as long as tax law requires.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to see the information we hold about you, have it corrected, have it deleted, get a copy to take elsewhere (the app’s “Back up all recipes” option does this for your cookbook), or object to how we use it. Email hello@spoonbook.co.uk and we’ll reply within a month. If you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Children

Spoonbook isn’t meant for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we’ll say so in the app. The date at the top shows when it last changed.
