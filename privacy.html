Spoonbook is a shared cookbook app run by [your name or business name] (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information Spoonbook keeps, why, and what you can do about it. We’ve kept it short and plain.

The short version

  • We keep your account and the recipes, photos and lists you put in your cookbook, so the app works and so the people you share with can see them.
  • We don’t show adverts, we don’t use analytics or tracking, and we don’t sell or share your information for marketing.
  • Spoonbook doesn’t use AI. Reading recipes, sorting the shopping list and working out calories all happen on your phone.

What we keep

  • Your account: your email address and password. Passwords are stored scrambled (hashed), so nobody can read them, including us.
  • Your name in the cookbook: the name you enter, so the people you share with can see who added what.
  • Your cookbook: recipes, notes, ratings, “we made this” dates, photos, meal plans, shopping lists, cupboard staples and usuals. Everyone who has joined your cookbook with its code can see and change these.
  • Payments (only if you buy Spoonbook Plus): whether your cookbook has Plus and when it renews. Payments are taken by our payment provider, [Lemon Squeezy], which acts as the seller and handles your card details. We never see your card number.

Where it’s kept

Your account and cookbook are stored with Supabase, our database and hosting provider, in their [region, e.g. London (eu-west-2)] data centre. The app itself is served by GitHub Pages. These providers process information only to run the service for us.

Spoonbook also keeps a copy of your cookbook and photos on your phone, so it opens quickly and works with no signal. Signing out removes the saved recipes from the phone.

Features that touch other services

  • Getting a recipe from a link: the web address you paste is fetched by our server so the recipe and its photo can be read. We keep only the recipe you choose to save.
  • Snapping a cookbook page: the photo is read on your phone. The first time you use it, your phone downloads the reading software from jsDelivr, a public file service, which sees your IP address like any website would.
  • Hands-free cooking: steps are read out and commands are heard using your phone’s built-in speech features. On many Android phones, speech recognition is provided by Google and may send audio to Google to turn it into text, under Google’s own privacy terms. The microphone is only used while hands-free is switched on in cooking mode.
  • Fonts: the app’s typefaces load from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address.
  • Calorie estimates use the UK Composition of Foods Integrated Dataset, built into the app. Nothing is sent anywhere to work them out.

Why we’re allowed to use it

We use your information to provide the app you’ve signed up for (performing our contract with you) and, for payments, to meet legal and tax duties. We don’t use it for anything else.

How long we keep it

We keep your account and cookbook for as long as you use Spoonbook. Deleted recipes stay in “Recently deleted” for 30 days, then they’re removed. If you ask us to delete your account, we’ll delete it and your cookbook within 30 days, unless other people still share that cookbook, in which case we remove you from it and leave their recipes with them. Copies can remain in our provider’s backups for a short time before they’re overwritten. Payment records are kept as long as tax law requires.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to see the information we hold about you, have it corrected, have it deleted, get a copy to take elsewhere (the app’s “Back up all recipes” option does this for your cookbook), or object to how we use it. Email [contact email] and we’ll reply within a month. If you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Children

Spoonbook isn’t meant for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we’ll say so in the app. The date at the top shows when it last changed.
